# ADR-002: Play Mode as a Toy-Den Metaphor (not a raw "laser button")

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-06-13

## Context

The original idea included a "laser button" that, when enabled, let the visitor
point a laser dot into page corners for Elis to chase. During clarification the
owner reframed this into a richer, more cohesive concept: instead of a single
abstract toggle, Elis should have her own **little den (toquinha)** from which
she fetches **toys** and leaves them available for the visitor to play with.

This reframes "play" from a feature toggle into an in-world, character-driven
ritual that fits the living-pet direction (ADR-001).

## Decision

Model play as a **toy-den metaphor**:

- Elis has a small den/house resting in a corner of the viewport.
- She can enter the den and bring out a **toy**, leaving it available in a
  "play corner" for the visitor.
- Supported toys (conceptually): a **ball**, a **laser dot**, and a
  **feather wand**. Each toy has its own play loop and chase/pounce/wiggle
  behaviors.
- Engaging is always the visitor's **choice** — picking up / moving a toy is
  what invites play. Nothing auto-starts a play session.
- Play is fully available on desktop (cursor) and adapted to touch on mobile
  (drag a toy with a finger).

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Raw Laser Toggle (original idea)

- **Description**: A button enables a laser the visitor moves to page corners
  for Elis to chase.
- **Pros**: Simplest to build; one mechanic.
- **Cons**: Feels mechanical and detached from the character; a glowing dot
  roaming the page risks distracting from reading; no narrative.
- **Why rejected**: The den-and-toys metaphor is more charming, on-brand, and
  makes play an intentional, contained ritual rather than an always-armed beam.

### Alternative 2: Always-Available Floating Toolbar of Toys

- **Description**: A persistent toy toolbar shown alongside Elis.
- **Pros**: Toys are immediately discoverable.
- **Cons**: Adds permanent UI chrome that competes with content; less magical
  than Elis fetching the toy herself.
- **Why rejected**: Conflicts with the reading-first, low-chrome aesthetic.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Play feels like part of Elis's world, reinforcing the living-pet identity.
- Toys are contained to a play corner, limiting on-page distraction.
- Extensible: new toys can be added as future delights.

### Negative

- More animation and asset work (den, multiple toys, per-toy behaviors).
- Discoverability of play must be handled gently without nagging the reader.

### Risks

- **Discoverability vs. intrusion** — mitigated by subtle, occasional,
  dismissible invitations rather than persistent prompts.
- **Per-toy scope** — mitigated by phasing toys (start with one, add more).

## Implementation Notes

Deferred to the TechSpec. Toy roster and per-toy behaviors should be phased;
the MVP need not ship all three toys at once.
