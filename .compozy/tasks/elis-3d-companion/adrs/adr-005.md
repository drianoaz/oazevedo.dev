# ADR-005: Baked Animation Clips on a Draco-Compressed GLB with Procedural Gaze

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-06-13

## Context

Elis is produced fresh in Blender from the references in `_referencias/`
(ADR-001). Two coupled decisions follow: how she is animated, and how the model
asset is delivered. The prototype ships a 513 KB **uncompressed** `cat.glb`
loaded via `useGLTF` with named clips (idle/jump/crouch/roll/wave) and a
procedural head rotation toward the cursor. On a reading-first site the model's
byte weight and decode cost matter, and the animation approach must be cheap to
produce while still reading as "alive".

## Decision

**Animation** — author a set of **baked animation clips** in the GLB
(idle, sleep, stretch, glance, walk, plus interaction/play clips added per
phase). The mood store (ADR-003) selects the target clip and the renderer
**crossfades** between clips via drei `useAnimations`. Continuous "alive"
signals that should track the visitor — head/eye gaze toward the cursor or
touch point — stay **procedural** (per-bone lerp in `useFrame`), layered on top
of whatever clip is playing.

**Asset delivery** — export the GLB with **Draco geometry compression**, loaded
via `useGLTF` configured with a `DracoLoader` (decoder served locally from
`public/`). The whole Elis subtree already loads under `dynamic({ ssr: false })`,
so the decoder fetch and decode happen off the critical reading path. Preload
the model after first paint, not before content.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Fully procedural animation

- **Description**: Generate all motion in code (springs/IK) from state, no baked
  clips.
- **Pros**: Maximal emergent fluidity.
- **Cons**: Far higher implementation cost and risk to likeness/charm within a
  reasonable timeline.
- **Why rejected**: Disproportionate effort for a secondary delight; baked clips
  give predictable, art-directed charm.

### Alternative 2: Uncompressed GLB (status quo)

- **Description**: Keep a plain GLB with no Draco/meshopt.
- **Pros**: Zero pipeline configuration.
- **Cons**: Larger download for a richer model than the prototype.
- **Why rejected**: Avoidable bytes on a performance-sensitive site when Draco
  is a one-time export setting.

### Alternative 3: meshopt compression

- **Description**: Compress with EXT_meshopt_compression and drei's decoder.
- **Pros**: Good size/decode balance.
- **Cons**: Another pipeline piece to maintain and validate on every Blender
  export.
- **Why rejected**: Draco is the more familiar, well-trodden path for this scale
  of model; meshopt remains a future option if needed.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Predictable, art-directed motion that reuses the proven drei clip pipeline.
- Smaller model download; decode cost kept off the reading critical path.
- Procedural gaze keeps Elis feeling responsive without bespoke clips.

### Negative

- A Draco decoder asset must be vendored in `public/` and wired into the loader.
- Animators must keep clip names stable as a contract with the selector code.

### Risks

- **Clip-name drift between Blender and code** — mitigated by a single typed map
  of clip names in `src/components/elis/`.
- **Rig lacks named gaze bones** — mitigated by agreeing the head/neck bone
  naming convention with the model export up front.

## Implementation Notes

Replaces `public/cat.glb` with a new Draco-compressed `public/elis.glb` (plus a
`public/draco/` decoder). The clip-name map and procedural-gaze code live in the
new `src/components/elis/` directory (see ADR-003 and the TechSpec).
