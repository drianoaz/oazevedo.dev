# ADR-006: Elis Model Construction — Low-Poly Box-Model with a Hand-Built Armature

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-06-13

## Context

ADR-001 requires a fresh Elis model produced in Blender from the references in
`_referencias/` (4 photos in `fotos/`, 4 drawings in `desenhos/`), in a cute,
chibi-like style that is recognizably the owner's real cat — calico coat, green
eyes, striped tail. ADR-005 fixes how the model is _delivered_ (baked clips on a
Draco GLB with procedural gaze) and depends on a stable clip-name and gaze-bone
contract. This ADR fixes how the model is _built and rigged_ — the production
pipeline upstream of that contract.

The model must be web-light (it ships to a reading-first site under a strict
performance budget), readable as charming rather than uncanny, and rigged with
named bones the runtime can drive (notably head/neck for the procedural gaze).

## Decision

Build Elis as a **low-poly chibi model via box-modeling**, hand-rigged:

- **Modeling** — box-model / extrusion from a primitive into a low-poly chibi
  silhouette guided by the photos (proportions, markings) and drawings (style).
  Keep the polycount deliberately low and the topology clean (quad-dominant,
  edge loops where deformation happens: neck, legs, tail).
- **UV + texture** — simple UV unwrap with a **hand-painted** texture for the
  calico coat, green eyes, and striped tail; flat/stylized shading consistent
  with the chibi look (no heavy PBR maps).
- **Rig** — a **lightweight hand-built armature** with explicitly **named
  bones**, including a dedicated **head/neck** chain for procedural gaze and
  the minimal limb/tail/spine bones needed for the Phase-1 clips. Bone names
  are the agreed contract with the runtime (ADR-005).
- **Export** — single mesh + armature exported to GLB with baked clips and
  Draco compression (ADR-005), replacing `public/cat.glb` with `public/elis.glb`.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Sculpt high-poly + retopology + bake

- **Description**: Sculpt at high resolution for likeness, retopologize to
  low-poly, bake normal/AO maps.
- **Pros**: Highest form fidelity.
- **Cons**: Longer pipeline; baked detail fights the flat chibi style and adds
  texture weight; higher uncanny risk for a "cute" target.
- **Why rejected**: Disproportionate for a stylized chibi cat; box-modeling
  reaches the desired look faster and lighter.

### Alternative 2: Generic cat base mesh + adjust

- **Description**: Start from a free cat base mesh/rig and retune proportions and
  texture toward Elis.
- **Pros**: Fastest path to something animated.
- **Cons**: Less unique; inherits topology/rig and bone naming we do not control,
  risking deformation and gaze-bone mismatches; weaker likeness/charm.
- **Why rejected**: ADR-001 explicitly wants a fresh, authentic, signature model;
  borrowed topology undermines both authenticity and the rig contract.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Web-light geometry and textures from the start, aligned with the budget.
- Full control of topology and bone naming → reliable deformation and a clean
  gaze-bone contract.
- Stylized hand-painted look matches the chibi/charm goal and avoids uncanny.

### Negative

- More manual modeling/rigging effort than reusing a base mesh.
- Likeness depends on the modeler's hand rather than sculpted scan fidelity.

### Risks

- **Likeness miss / uncanny** — mitigated by iterating against `_referencias/`
  with owner sign-off before broad rollout (PRD risk); prototype idle + sleep
  early to validate read.
- **Deformation artifacts at low poly** — mitigated by placing edge loops at
  deformation zones (neck, legs, tail) during modeling.

## Implementation Notes

Deliverable: `public/elis.glb` (low-poly, hand-painted, Draco) plus a documented
bone-name and clip-name list shared with the runtime. Clip authoring and the
mood→clip mapping are covered in ADR-007.
