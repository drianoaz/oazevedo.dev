# ADR-001: Elis as a Living Pet Simulation

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-06-13

## Context

oazevedo.dev is a reading-first personal blog. The owner wants a unique,
signature element — a 3D model of his real cat "Elis" — that lives on the site
and interacts with visitors. The element is explicitly a **secondary delight**:
it must never compete with or disrupt the primary content (blog reading).

Three experiential models were weighed for how "alive" Elis should feel:

1. A curated, event-driven reactive character (scripted states).
2. A living pet simulation with an internal mood/energy model and emergent,
   semi-autonomous behavior.
3. A mostly-static cute presence with richness hidden behind an opt-in widget.

The owner stated this is "the feature I most want to develop," signaling
appetite for ambition and willingness to invest, while still requiring that
reading is never harmed.

## Decision

Build Elis as a **living pet simulation**: she has an internal state
(mood/energy, e.g., playful, sleepy, curious, bored) that drives
semi-autonomous behavior over time — idling, napping on her own schedule,
glancing at the cursor/touch point, wandering at the viewport edges, and
occasionally seeking attention. Discrete visitor actions (cursor/touch
movement, clicking her, scrolling, picking up a toy) feed into this state and
shape her reactions, rather than triggering isolated scripted clips.

This ambition is bounded by **non-negotiable reading guardrails** (see ADR-002
companion constraints in the PRD): she auto-quiets during active reading,
honors `prefers-reduced-motion`, is dismissible with a persisted preference,
stays within a fixed performance budget, and never captures focus or scroll
from page content.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Curated Reactive Companion

- **Description**: A fixed set of well-defined animation states triggered by
  discrete events; predictable and easy to polish.
- **Pros**: Lowest effort and risk; trivially keeps out of the reader's way;
  easy to phase.
- **Cons**: Behavior feels scripted, not alive; lacks the "she really lives
  here" magic the owner is after.
- **Why rejected**: Delivers competence but not the signature, memorable
  presence the owner explicitly prioritized.

### Alternative 2: On-Demand Play Corner

- **Description**: Elis is a mostly-static cute presence; interactive richness
  is concentrated in a "play with Elis" widget the visitor opens.
- **Pros**: Minimal distraction risk; richness is fully opt-in.
- **Cons**: Loses the ambient "she lives on the site" feeling; most visitors
  never experience the depth.
- **Why rejected**: Conflicts with the owner's goal of an ambient companion
  with a life of her own.

## Consequences

### Positive

- A genuinely memorable, signature experience that differentiates the blog.
- Emergent behavior keeps repeat visits fresh and rewarding.
- Strong demonstration of front-end / 3D / interaction craft.

### Negative

- Highest production effort: behavior/state model, richer rig, and a larger
  library of animations.
- More surface area for behavior to feel "off" or to distract if guardrails
  are weak.

### Risks

- **Distraction risk** — mitigated by mandatory reading guardrails
  (auto-quiet, reduced-motion, dismissible, performance budget).
- **Scope creep** — mitigated by phased rollout: ship ambient life first,
  then play interactions, then full toy-fetching and chase behaviors.
- **Performance on weak devices** — mitigated by a strict performance budget
  and graceful degradation (including mobile/touch adaptation).
